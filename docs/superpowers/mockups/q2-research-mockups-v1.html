Q2 · Research · 16 marks

Research, inside out.

Question 2 never asks what you think about the issue. It asks how we would know. In 2(a) you inspect someone else's research; in 2(b) you design your own. You'll use the same skills again in your Individual Report and your Team Project.

Direct link: the same skill, marked againSupporting linkTap any node to isolate its links

Q2(a) ⇄ C2 Table E

Inspecting research is evaluating sources.

In 2(a) you judge a stranger's interview. In your Report you judge the websites and studies you cite. Either way you name a feature, show how it affects the evidence, and say why that matters for the purpose.

Same sentence shape: "Because ___, the evidence may be ___, so it is less useful for finding out ___."

Q2(b) ⇄ C3 Table A

Testing a claim is planning evidence.

Table A rewards a plan that shows "how the action will be evidenced and how its success will be measured". That is a 2(b) research design pointed at your own project.

Same matrix: Who will you ask? How? What will you find out? Why does it prove success?

Q2 ⇄ C1 Q3

One reliability checklist, four questions.

Expertise, accuracy, bias, vested interest, "reason to lie?". The checklist you use to inspect research in 2(a) is the one you use to judge arguments in Q3.

Learn it once in the Toolkit. It appears in 2(a), Q3, C2 and C3.

Q2(a) · Evaluate research

The Inspection.

"Explain the strengths and weaknesses of the research outlined in Source 3." You are marking the method, not the topic. Each point needs three links: what the researcher did, what that does to the evidence, and why it matters for what they were trying to find out.

OverviewToolkitThe moveTrapsWorked examplesPracticeChecklist

Find it, then follow it through.

Tap a highlighted phrase. Teal phrases help the research and red ones weaken it, but you still have to explain why. That explanation is where the marks are.

Source 3: A student's projectJUNE 2026 · 0457/12 · INSERT

While studying international law, I wanted to research the amount of vehicle crimeS1. I contacted a local building firmW1 to arrange an interview.

Signing a confidentiality documentS2 gave me the opportunity to ask questions. I asked Mr Grant, the manager, permission to record the meetingS3. Mr Grant had only worked at the company for one monthW2 but was happy to talk during his coffee break.

The building firm's café was busy and noisyW3. …

W1 · "a local building firm"Weakness
1 · Feature

The only data comes from one local building firm.

"The researcher only…"

2 · Effect on evidence

One small, unrepresentative sample: a builder's experience of van theft can't show a national pattern.

"This means the evidence may be…"

3 · Purpose link

The aim was the amount of vehicle crime, so the conclusion about national trends is not supported.

"…so it is less useful for finding out…"

Q2(b) · Test a claim

The Test Bench.

"Explain how this claim could be tested. You should consider the research methods and evidence that could be used." Don't argue for or against the claim, and don't critique your own methods. Design the investigation and justify each choice.

OverviewToolkitSplit the claimThe matrixWorked examplesPracticeChecklist

Three rows. Three developed points.

The claim · June 2026 · 0457/12

"The amount of vehicle crime is increasing nationally."

1 WhatHow much vehicle crime is there? You need a measurable quantity.
2 Change"Increasing" means comparing over time, so you need data from past years.
3 Scope"Nationally", not in one town, so you need nationwide coverage.
